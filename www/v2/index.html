A FIRST DRAFT

A map of space.
And a view into time.

Start with our observable universe, then explore inward to the Milky Way. All objects live in one 3D coordinate system. Rotating the map reveals depth; the distances are not flattened.

What is measured?

Named Local Group galaxies and cluster centers use the existing atlas’s approximate catalog distances and directions. The sample is incomplete. Cluster clouds, spiral arms, and the wider cosmic web are illustrations, not a galaxy survey.

What does time mean here?

Faraway regions are seen earlier in cosmic history. The observer stays at our location even as you move the camera. The outer bands are schematic eras, not physical shells. The CMB marks the oldest light we see, just inside the observable horizon.

Distance is expressed in comoving coordinates at large scales, using a flat ΛCDM model (H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.315). Nearby distances are the approximate measured values. Screen separations are projections of the full 3D distances.

Explore

Drag to rotate; scroll to zoom; Shift-drag to pan. On touchscreens, use one finger to rotate and two to zoom and pan. With the map focused, use arrow keys, + / −, and R to reset. Find an object, then choose “Explore here” to make it your focus.
